A systematic review with meta-analysis in the Journal of Hand Therapy investigated a proximal approach: adding scapular muscle strengthening to conventional physiotherapy. The review compared this combined approach against conventional physiotherapy alone, looking at outcomes for pain, grip strength, and function. The idea of addressing the kinetic chain is certainly compelling. But again, we must be cautious. A systematic review in Sports Medicine examined whether biomechanical alterations during walking persist after ACL reconstruction, and whether the time between injury and surgery influences these patterns. This is a really practical question for those of us guiding patients back to normal gait. The review highlights that surgical timing may be a modifier of walking biomechanics. Shifting to amputee rehabilitation, a systematic review in the Journal of Rehabilitation Medicine synthesized evidence on extended reality, or XR, serious games. The most interesting finding was that augmented reality-based phantom motor execution reported the largest effect sizes, with a Cohen's d between zero point nine and one point three. But the review notes heterogeneous study designs and that some of the functional outcome evidence is preliminary. So, XR is a tool to consider, particularly for phantom pain, but we need more rigorous trials to confirm its role in functional recovery.

Next, a study directly relevant to spinal cord injury rehabilitation. A meta-analysis in The Journal of Spinal Cord Medicine looked at transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation, or TENS, for managing spasticity. The pooled results showed a significant reduction in clinical spasticity scores, with a standardized mean difference of minus two point zero eight. That's a large effect. However, this finding comes with a major caveat: the analysis included only seven studies and there was substantial heterogeneity between them. This means the effect is not uniform across all patients or protocols. TENS appears to be a potential noninvasive adjunct, but the high variability limits our confidence in a one-size-fits-all approach.
